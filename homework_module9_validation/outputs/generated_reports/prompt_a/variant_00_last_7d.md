## Earthquake Activity Summary: Feb 9–16, 2026

- **38 earthquakes** were recorded globally over the 7-day period, with activity **accelerating toward the end of the week** — daily counts climbed from 4–5 events early on to a peak of **9 events on Feb 14**, suggesting a possible regional swarm or heightened tectonic stress.

- **Magnitudes ranged from M4.0 to M5.7**, with an average of M4.56 — a moderate but broadly elevated baseline indicating sustained seismic unrest across multiple fault systems rather than a single isolated sequence.

- **Tobelo, Indonesia was the week's most active hotspot**, logging 5 events including the **largest quake of the period (M5.7 on Feb 14)** — this cluster warrants close attention, as repeated stress releases in the same zone can sometimes precede larger ruptures.

- **Shallow-depth events pose elevated local hazard** in several locations: a M4.8 near Tual, Indonesia struck at just 2 km depth, a M5.2 off Honshu, Japan at 2.7 km, and multiple events near Mohr, Iran and Hachinohe, Japan were also very shallow (under 10 km), increasing surface shaking potential.

- **No tsunami warnings were triggered** across any of the 38 events, including the deeper high-magnitude quakes in the Banda Sea (M5.6, 177 km) and Kuril Islands (M5.3, 113 km) — depth appears to have buffered the most energetic events from generating sea-level disruption.

- **Watch closely:** The **Tobelo/Indonesia cluster**, ongoing activity near **Nikolski, Alaska** (4 events), and the **Mohr, Iran sequence** (3 events including a M4.9 at shallow depth) should all be monitored for escalation — any uptick in frequency or a jump above M6.0 in these zones would signal significantly heightened risk.
