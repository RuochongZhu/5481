## Earthquake Activity Summary: Jan 17 – Feb 16, 2026

- **150 earthquakes** were recorded over the 30-day period, averaging **5 events per day**, with the busiest days falling on February 3 and February 14 (9 events each), suggesting episodic clustering rather than steady background activity.

- **Magnitudes ranged from M4.0 to M6.2**, with an average of M4.62 — the upper end of that range (M6.2) is strong enough to cause localized damage and warrants close attention to where it struck and what infrastructure was nearby.

- **Tobelo, Indonesia led all regions with 19 events**, followed by Mohr, Iran (15), Nikolski, Alaska (13), Hachinohe, Japan (11), and Lata, Solomon Islands (10) — a geographically diverse but recognizable set of seismically active zones along major tectonic plate boundaries.

- **Several shallow events are a high-risk signal**: events near Tual, Indonesia (depth 2.0 km), Hachinohe, Japan (4.1 km), and off Honshu, Japan (2.7 km) occurred very close to the surface, where ground shaking is more intense and damage potential is significantly higher even at moderate magnitudes.

- **No tsunami warnings were triggered** across any of the 30 sample events logged, including those in known tsunami-prone areas such as the Solomon Islands, Indonesia, and Alaska — a reassuring finding, though shallow coastal events always merit ongoing vigilance.

- **Watch closely in the coming weeks**: the Tobelo–Indonesia cluster, Iran's Mohr region (which saw multiple shallow strikes), and the Aleutian/Alaska corridor all showed persistent, repeated activity — patterns that can sometimes precede larger events or indicate ongoing fault stress accumulation.
