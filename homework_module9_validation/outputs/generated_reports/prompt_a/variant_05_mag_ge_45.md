## Earthquake Activity Summary: Jan 17 – Feb 16, 2026
*(M4.5+ events | Source: USGS Synthetic)*

- **📊 Event Volume:** A total of **79 earthquakes** of magnitude 4.5 or greater were recorded over the 31-day period, averaging roughly **2–3 events per day**, with a notable spike of **7 events on February 3** — the single busiest day of the window.

- **📏 Magnitude Range:** Quakes ranged from **M4.5 to M6.2**, with an average magnitude of **M5.01**, indicating a steady drumbeat of moderate seismicity without a dominant mainshock driving the sequence.

- **🌍 Most Active Regions:** **Tobelo, Indonesia** led all areas with **11 events**, followed by **Nikolski, Alaska (9)**, **Mohr, Iran (7)**, **La Tirana, Chile (6)**, and **Hachinohe, Japan (5)** — together accounting for nearly half of all recorded activity.

- **🌊 Tsunami Alert Triggered:** One event stands out as a high-risk signal — a **M5.7 quake near the Kermadec Islands on February 8** at a very shallow depth of just **5.7 km** did generate a **tsunami warning**, making it the only tsunami-flagged event in the dataset and a key concern for the broader Pacific region.

- **⚠️ Shallow-Depth Risk Signals:** Several events occurred at critically shallow depths (under 10 km), including quakes near **Tual, Indonesia (2.0 km)**, **Tobelo, Indonesia (3.3 km)**, **Nikolski, Alaska (1.4 km)**, and **off Honshu, Japan (1.8 km)** — shallow events amplify surface shaking and tsunami potential even at moderate magnitudes.

- **🔭 What to Monitor Next:** Watch **Tobelo, Indonesia** and the **Nikolski/Aleutian Islands corridor** closely, as both showed persistent, multi-week clustering that can precede larger events. The **Kermadec Islands zone** warrants continued tsunami readiness given the February 8 trigger, and **Mohr, Iran** — a densely populated region — should be tracked given its 7 events, several at seismically dangerous shallow-to-mid crustal depths.
