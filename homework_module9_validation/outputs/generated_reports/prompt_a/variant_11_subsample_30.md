## Earthquake Activity Summary: Jan 17 – Feb 16, 2026

- **45 recorded events** occurred over the 31-day window, averaging roughly 1–2 earthquakes per day, with the busiest single day being February 3 (4 events). Activity was broadly distributed but concentrated along Pacific Rim and Southeast Asian fault zones.

- **Magnitudes ranged from M4.0 to M5.7**, with an average of M4.63 — a moderately active period sitting above routine background levels. Three separate events reached the M5.7 peak: Tobelo, Indonesia (Feb 14); San Alejandro, Peru (Feb 5); and Mariana Islands region (Jan 28).

- **Tobelo, Indonesia was the clear hotspot**, accounting for 8 of the 45 events (18%) — including a shallow M5.3 at just 21 km depth and the period's joint-highest M5.7 at intermediate depth (153 km). This cluster warrants close attention given the region's history of damaging seismicity.

- **One tsunami alert was triggered**: a M5.7 strike near the Kermadec Islands on February 8, at only 5.7 km depth. Shallow-focus events in oceanic subduction zones carry elevated tsunami generation potential, even at moderate magnitudes.

- **Additional high-risk signals** include a M4.8 event in Tual, Indonesia at just 2 km depth (Feb 14) and a M4.8 near San Alejandro, Peru at 3.8 km — both very shallow events capable of causing localized surface damage disproportionate to their magnitude.

- **Watch closely**: the Tobelo–Tual corridor (Indonesia), the Kermadec–Tonga subduction zone for further shallow events with tsunami potential, and the Mariana Islands region (4 events, including a M5.4), all of which showed sustained or escalating activity toward the end of the reporting period.
