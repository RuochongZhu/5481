## Global Earthquake Activity Summary: Feb 2–15, 2026

- **70 earthquakes** were recorded over the 14-day window, averaging **5 events per day**, with notable activity spikes on Feb 3 (9 events) and Feb 14 (9 events) — suggesting possible cluster sequences rather than random background seismicity.

- **Magnitude range spanned M4.0 to M6.1**, with an average of M4.63. The M6.1 peak represents a meaningful threshold — strong enough to cause light-to-moderate damage near populated areas, depending on depth and proximity.

- **Nikolski, Alaska and Tobelo, Indonesia led all regions with 8 events each**, followed by Mohr, Iran and La Tirana, Chile (6 each). This geographic spread across four major tectonic boundaries signals broad, simultaneous fault-system stress.

- **High-risk signals worth flagging:** Several shallow events (under 10 km depth) were recorded near Mohr, Iran (M4.1 at 1.9 km, M4.9 at 8.5 km), Tual, Indonesia (M4.8 at 2.0 km), and off Honshu, Japan (M5.2 at 2.7 km) — shallow quakes amplify surface shaking and structural damage potential significantly.

- **No tsunami warnings were triggered** across any of the 30 sampled events, including the deeper high-magnitude strikes in the Banda Sea (M5.6) and Tobelo (M5.7), likely due to their intermediate-to-deep focal depths reducing seafloor displacement.

- **Monitor closely:** The Tobelo–Banda Sea corridor and the Mohr, Iran cluster for aftershock escalation; the late-period uptick (Feb 13–15 combined: 22 events) may indicate a building sequence — any event exceeding **M6.5 in these zones** should trigger immediate reassessment.
