## Earthquake Activity Summary: Jan 17 – Feb 16, 2026
*(M4.0+ events | Source: USGS Synthetic)*

- **📊 Overall Activity:** A total of **150 earthquakes** of magnitude 4.0 or greater were recorded over the 31-day period, averaging roughly **5 events per day** — with the busiest single days being February 3 and February 14, each logging **9 events**.

- **📏 Magnitude Range:** Quakes ranged from **M4.0 to M6.2**, with an average magnitude of **4.62**. The lone M6.2 event represents the most significant release of energy in the period and warrants close review of its location and any reported impacts.

- **🌍 Hotspot Regions:** The five most active areas were **Tobelo, Indonesia (19 events)**, **Mohr, Iran (15)**, **Nikolski, Alaska (13)**, **Hachinohe, Japan (11)**, and **Lata, Solomon Islands (10)** — all situated on major tectonic plate boundaries or known fault systems.

- **⚠️ High-Risk Signals:** Several shallow events stand out as elevated-risk: a **M4.8 near Tual, Indonesia at just 2.0 km depth**, a **M4.1 near Mohr, Iran at 1.9 km**, and a **M5.2 off Honshu, Japan at 2.7 km**. Shallow quakes amplify surface shaking and structural damage potential even at moderate magnitudes.

- **🌊 Tsunami Threat:** Despite activity in tsunami-prone zones — including the Solomon Islands, Indonesia, Alaska, and Tonga — **no tsunami alerts were triggered** during this period. Continued vigilance is warranted given the persistent seismicity in Pacific island and subduction-zone regions.

- **🔭 What to Watch Next:** Monitor **Tobelo, Indonesia** for escalating cluster activity, which could signal stress accumulation ahead of a larger event. Track **Mohr, Iran** for shallow aftershock progression, and keep an eye on **Nikolski, Alaska** and the **Kuril–Japan arc**, where deep and shallow events are occurring in close succession — a pattern sometimes preceding stronger mainshocks.
