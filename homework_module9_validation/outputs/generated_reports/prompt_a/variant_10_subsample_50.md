## Earthquake Activity Summary: Jan 17 – Feb 16, 2026

- **75 earthquakes** were recorded over the 31-day period, averaging roughly 2–3 events per day, with the busiest single days on January 23 (5 events) and the opening day, January 17 (4 events).

- **Magnitudes ranged from M4.0 to M6.2**, with an average of M4.62, indicating a period dominated by moderate seismicity — but the M6.2 peak warrants attention as it crosses the threshold for potential structural damage in vulnerable areas.

- **Mohr, Iran led all regions with 10 events**, followed closely by Nikolski, Alaska (9) and Tobelo, Indonesia (8) — all three are seismically active zones sitting atop major fault systems, making this clustering noteworthy rather than coincidental.

- **One tsunami alert was triggered**: a M5.7 strike near the Kermadec Islands on Feb 8 at shallow depth (5.7 km) generated a tsunami flag — the only such signal in the dataset and a reminder that even sub-M6 shallow events in subduction zones carry real hazard.

- **Several shallow, high-risk events stand out**: a M4.5 near Tobelo at just 3.3 km depth (Feb 14), a M4.9 in Mohr, Iran at 8.5 km (Feb 12), and a M5.5 near La Tirana, Chile at 8.9 km (Feb 9) — shallow earthquakes amplify surface shaking and aftershock potential significantly.

- **Watch closely in the coming weeks**: the Mohr, Iran cluster (10 events, multiple shallow strikes) and the Tobelo, Indonesia corridor (8 events, varying depths) show sustained activity that could signal stress accumulation; any uptick in magnitude or frequency in these zones should be treated as an early warning signal.
