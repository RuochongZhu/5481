## Global Seismic Activity Summary: Jan 17 – Feb 16, 2026
*(M5.0+ events only | Source: USGS Synthetic)*

- **39 significant earthquakes** were recorded over the 31-day period, averaging roughly 1–2 events per active day, with the busiest single day being January 27 (4 events). Activity was fairly steady throughout, with a modest uptick in the final days of the window (3 events on February 15).

- **Magnitudes ranged from M5.0 to M6.2**, with an average of M5.33 — indicating a period dominated by moderate shaking, though three events crossed the M6.0 threshold (M6.2 Nikolski, Alaska; M6.1 La Tirana, Chile ×2), which are capable of causing localized damage.

- **Tobelo, Indonesia was the most active hotspot**, producing 6 events across the period — the highest concentration of any single region — followed by La Tirana, Chile (4 events) and the Mariana Islands region (4 events), signaling persistent stress along these subduction zones.

- **Four events triggered tsunami alerts**, the most notable being a M5.7 near the Kermadec Islands (depth: 5.7 km, Feb 8), a M5.5 in the New Britain region of PNG (depth: 7.4 km, Feb 3), and a M5.5 near Tual, Indonesia (depth: 5.4 km, Jan 27) — all shallow-focus quakes where seafloor displacement risk is highest.

- **Shallow-depth events are the primary concern**: several earthquakes struck at depths under 10 km (including events off Honshu at 1.8–2.7 km and Lata, Solomon Islands at 1.1 km), making them disproportionately dangerous for surface shaking and tsunami generation despite their moderate magnitudes.

- **Watch closely in the coming weeks**: Tobelo (Indonesia), the Kermadec–Tonga arc, and La Tirana (Chile) should remain under close monitoring given their elevated event frequency and/or shallow seismicity; any escalation toward M6.5+ in these zones could signal a broader seismic sequence or foreshock activity.
