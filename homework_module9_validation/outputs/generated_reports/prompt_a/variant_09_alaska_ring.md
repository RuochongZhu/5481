## Alaska Seismic Activity Summary: Jan 17 – Feb 16, 2026

- **16 earthquakes** were recorded across Alaska's Aleutian region over the 30-day window, averaging roughly one event every two days — with a modest uptick in frequency during the final week (4 events across Feb 14–15 alone).

- **Magnitudes ranged from M4.0 to M6.2**, with an average of M4.71, indicating a period of persistent moderate seismicity rather than isolated background noise.

- **The M6.2 event near Nikolski on January 25** is the standout signal — the strongest quake in the dataset. Though no tsunami was triggered, a shallow follow-on sequence east of Nikolski (depths as low as 1.0–5.8 km) in the weeks after warrants attention as potential aftershock activity.

- **Nikolski, Alaska dominated the activity**, accounting for 13 of 16 events (81%). Several of these were extremely shallow (under 5 km depth), which can amplify ground shaking and structural risk for local communities even at moderate magnitudes.

- **No tsunami alerts were issued** for any event in this period, including the M6.2 — a reassuring sign, though the Aleutian subduction zone remains one of the world's most tsunami-capable environments.

- **Watch closely for:** continued shallow seismicity east of Nikolski, any event exceeding M6.5 in this cluster, and whether the late-February acceleration in daily counts (2 events on both Feb 14 and Feb 15) signals a building sequence rather than tapering aftershocks.
