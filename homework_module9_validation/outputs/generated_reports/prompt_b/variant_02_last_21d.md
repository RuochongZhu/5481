# Global Earthquake Report: Jan 26 – Feb 16, 2026

## Executive Summary
A total of 106 earthquakes of magnitude 4.0 or greater rattled the globe over the past three weeks, with the strongest clocking in at M6.1. Activity was broadly distributed across the Pacific Rim and Middle East, with no tsunami alerts triggered during the period.

## Key Observations
- **Most active hotspot:** Tobelo, Indonesia led all regions with 12 events, followed closely by Nikolski, Alaska (11) and Mohr, Iran (10) — together accounting for roughly a third of all recorded quakes.
- **Strongest shakers:** The period's largest events included an M5.7 near Tobelo, an M5.6 in the Banda Sea, and an M5.3 off Kuril'sk, Russia — all significant but below the threshold typically associated with widespread destruction.
- **Activity spike late in the period:** Daily counts surged to 9 events on both February 3 and February 14, suggesting possible cluster sequences or aftershock activity in one or more regions.
- **Shallow quakes of note:** Several events occurred at very shallow depths (under 10 km) near Tual, Indonesia (2.0 km), Mohr, Iran (1.9 km), and Hachinohe, Japan (1.6 km) — shallow quakes tend to produce stronger ground shaking at the surface even at moderate magnitudes.

## Risk Watch
- ⚠️ **Tobelo & Banda Sea, Indonesia:** Persistent shallow and mid-depth activity in this tectonically complex zone warrants close monitoring; a future larger event could affect coastal communities and trigger localized tsunami risk.
- ⚠️ **Mohr, Iran:** Ten events in three weeks, including very shallow quakes (under 10 km), in a region with dense population and older building stock — conditions that amplify casualty risk if magnitudes escalate.
- ⚠️ **Nikolski, Alaska (Aleutian Arc):** Eleven events reflect ongoing stress along one of the world's most seismically productive subduction zones, which has a historical record of generating large tsunamigenic earthquakes.
