# Global Earthquake Activity Report
**Period: January 17 – February 16, 2026**

---

## Executive Summary
A total of 75 significant earthquakes (M4.0–M6.2) rattled seismically active zones across the globe over the past 31 days, averaging roughly 2–3 events per day. Activity was concentrated along well-known fault systems in Iran, Alaska, Indonesia, and Japan, with one tsunami-generating event recorded near the Kermadec Islands. No major metropolitan areas reported catastrophic damage during this period.

---

## Key Observations
- 🌍 **Mohr, Iran led all hotspots** with 10 recorded events, including shallow quakes as close as 1.9 km to the surface — depths that typically amplify ground shaking and structural damage risk.
- 🌊 **The period's strongest quake (M6.2)** occurred somewhere in the dataset, while a notable **M5.7 near the Kermadec Islands on Feb. 8** was the only event to trigger a tsunami warning (no reported harm).
- 📈 **Activity spiked in late January** (Jan. 23–24 saw 9 combined events) before settling into a steadier pace through February, suggesting a possible cluster sequence rather than uniform background seismicity.
- 🗾 **Japan and Alaska were persistently active**, with Hachinohe (7 events) and Nikolski (9 events) appearing repeatedly — consistent with their positions on the Pacific Ring of Fire.

## Risk Watch
- ⚠️ **Mohr, Iran deserves close monitoring:** Multiple shallow quakes (some under 2 km deep) in a region with older building stock raise concerns about cumulative structural stress and aftershock vulnerability.
- ⚠️ **Kermadec Islands zone remains tsunami-capable:** The Feb. 8 M5.7 shallow-focus event (5.7 km depth) confirmed this subduction corridor can generate sea-level disturbances even at moderate magnitudes — coastal communities in New Zealand and Tonga should maintain alert protocols.
- ⚠️ **Tobelo, Indonesia (8 events) showed mixed depth profiles**, including a very shallow M4.5 at just 3.3 km — shallow events in this densely populated island region carry elevated local damage potential even at lower magnitudes.
