# Earthquake Situation Report

## Executive Summary
16 seismic events were recorded across Alaska between 2026-01-17 and 2026-02-16, with Nikolski, Alaska accounting for 13 of those events and the Aleutian Islands, Alaska accounting for the remaining 3. The period's maximum magnitude of 6.2 at Nikolski on 2026-01-25, combined with persistent shallow seismicity in the same cluster, represents the headline risk signal for this reporting window.

## Top Signals
- **Nikolski dominates event frequency**: 13 of 16 total events were centered at or near Nikolski, Alaska, indicating a sustained and geographically concentrated seismic cluster over the 30-day window.
- **M6.2 peak event on 2026-01-25**: The largest recorded event (magnitude 6.2, depth 124.0 km) struck Nikolski, Alaska — the single highest-magnitude event in the dataset and a benchmark for assessing structural and community impact in that area.
- **Shallow crustal events near Nikolski suggest elevated surface hazard**: Multiple events recorded at depths of 5.8 km or less (including a M4.9 at 1.4 km depth on 2026-02-05 and a M4.8 at 5.8 km on 2026-02-08) suggest shallow fault activity that may produce stronger ground shaking relative to their magnitudes.
- **Accelerating event rate in late period**: 6 events occurred in the final 5 days of the window (2026-02-11 through 2026-02-15), compared to 10 events spread across the preceding 25 days, indicating a recent uptick in activity frequency around the Nikolski–Aleutian Islands corridor.

## Risk Watch (Next 7 Days)
- **Nikolski, Alaska (M4.0–M6.2 band)**: This cluster warrants continued close monitoring given 13 events in 30 days, including multiple shallow events and one M6.2. Any escalation in magnitude or depth shallowing within this zone should trigger reassessment of local infrastructure and population exposure.
- **Aleutian Islands corridor (M4.0+ band)**: The 3 events recorded in this sub-region, including a M5.2 at 65 km E of the Aleutian Islands on 2026-02-15, suggest this adjacent zone merits parallel monitoring as a potential extension of the Nikolski cluster's spatial footprint.
- **Shallow-depth events (≤10 km) across both regions**: The concentration of very shallow events (minimum recorded depth 1.0 km) near Nikolski indicates that even moderate-magnitude events in this depth range could produce disproportionate ground motion; monitoring agencies should flag any new shallow detections for rapid review.

## Data Caveats
- **Data window**: This report covers events from 2026-01-17 through 2026-02-16 (30 days), filtered exclusively to Alaska-region events as defined by the source query; activity outside this geographic filter is not represented.
- **Dataset limitations**: The source is labeled "usgs-synthetic," which may indicate this is a simulated or test dataset rather than verified observational data; coordinators should confirm data provenance with USGS before operationalizing any findings. Additionally, only 16 sample events are present, and it is not derivable from current data whether this represents the complete catalog or a subset.

## Recommended Monitoring Actions
1. **Verify infrastructure status at Nikolski, Alaska within the next 24 hours**: Contact Alaska Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management to confirm whether the M6.2 event on 2026-01-25 and subsequent shallow events have prompted any structural assessments of critical facilities (utilities, communications, transportation links) serving the Nikolski community.
2. **Establish a magnitude threshold alert for the Nikolski–Aleutian Islands corridor**: Coordinate with the Alaska Earthquake Center to ensure automated notifications are active for any new events at M4.5 or above within this cluster, given the recent uptick in event frequency and the presence of very shallow seismicity (≤5.8 km depth).
3. **Confirm tsunami warning system status for the Aleutian Islands zone**: Although no tsunami flags were recorded for any of the 16 events in this dataset, the Aleutian Islands corridor is a known tsunami-generation environment; coordinators should verify within 24 hours that the Pacific Tsunami Warning Center's monitoring posture for this region is current and that local notification protocols for coastal communities are up to date.
