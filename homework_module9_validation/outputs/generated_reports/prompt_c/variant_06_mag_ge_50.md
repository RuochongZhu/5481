# Earthquake Situation Report

## Executive Summary
39 seismic events of magnitude 5.0 or greater were recorded globally between 2026-01-17 and 2026-02-16. Tobelo, Indonesia led all regions with 6 events, followed by La Tirana, Chile and the Mariana Islands region, each with 4 events. The period's headline risk signal is the concentration of shallow, tsunami-flagged events across the western Pacific, combined with the period's maximum magnitude of 6.2 recorded at Nikolski, Alaska.

## Top Signals
- **Tobelo, Indonesia** registered 6 events — the highest regional frequency in the digest — including a magnitude 5.7 at 152.9 km depth on 2026-02-14 and a magnitude 5.3 at only 21.4 km depth on 2026-02-04, indicating both deep and shallow seismic activity in the same zone.
- **La Tirana, Chile** produced 4 events including two magnitude 6.1 earthquakes (2026-01-30 at 96.9 km depth and 2026-02-02 at 184.2 km depth), making it the highest-magnitude cluster outside Alaska during this period.
- **Three events in the sample dataset were flagged tsunami: true** — the magnitude 5.7 at 55 km E of Kermadec Islands (depth 5.7 km, 2026-02-08), the magnitude 5.5 in the New Britain region, PNG (depth 7.4 km, 2026-02-03), and the magnitude 5.5 at 30 km E of Tual, Indonesia (depth 5.4 km, 2026-01-27) — all three at shallow depths under 10 km, consistent with elevated tsunami generation potential.
- The period's strongest event, a **magnitude 6.2 at Nikolski, Alaska** on 2026-01-25 (depth 124.0 km), occurred in the Aleutian arc, a region that also produced a magnitude 5.1 at only 14.2 km depth on 2026-02-14, suggesting continued stress release across a depth range in this subduction zone.

## Risk Watch (Next 7 Days)
- **Tobelo, Indonesia** warrants priority monitoring given 6 recorded events across a wide depth range (21.4–152.9 km) within the 30-day window; coordinators should track any shallowing of activity, as events under 30 km depth in this region have previously been associated with tsunami flags elsewhere in the digest.
- **Kermadec Islands and New Britain, PNG** should remain under elevated watch: both produced shallow (under 10 km depth), tsunami-flagged events during the digest period, and the Kermadec event at 5.7 km depth suggests fault rupture close to the seafloor — a configuration that warrants sustained Pacific-basin tsunami monitoring.
- **La Tirana, Chile** produced 4 events including two magnitude 6.1 earthquakes in a 3-day window (2026-01-30 to 2026-02-02); the magnitude band 5.5–6.2 across the northern Chile subduction segment should be monitored for continued elevated activity, particularly given the region's proximity to populated Andean communities.

## Data Caveats
- The digest covers a 30-day window from 2026-01-17 to 2026-02-16 and applies a minimum magnitude filter of 5.0; events below this threshold — which may be relevant for assessing aftershock sequences or swarm behavior — are excluded from this dataset.
- The provider is identified as "usgs-synthetic," indicating this dataset may not reflect real-world seismic records; operational decisions should be validated against authoritative real-time feeds (e.g., USGS ANSS ComCat, PTWC) before resource deployment. Additionally, the sample events section contains 30 of the 39 total events; full event-level details for the remaining 9 events are not derivable from current data.

2. **Brief regional liaisons in Indonesia (North Maluku/Tobelo area) and Chile (Tarapacá region/La Tirana)** on the elevated event counts in their jurisdictions — 6 and 4 events respectively — and request status updates on local infrastructure, emergency response posture, and any felt-shaking reports from the past 30 days.
3. **Establish a magnitude threshold alert** (suggested: M ≥ 5.5) on real-time monitoring platforms for the Aleutian arc (Nikolski area), Tobelo, and the Kermadec–PNG corridor, and designate a duty officer to review any triggers within the next 24 hours to ensure rapid escalation protocols are current.
