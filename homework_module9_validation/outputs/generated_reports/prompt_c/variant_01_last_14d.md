70 seismic events were recorded between 2026-02-02 and 2026-02-15, ranging from magnitude 4.0 to 6.1 with an average magnitude of 4.63. - **Mohr, Iran** contributed 6 events, including a magnitude 4.9 at 8.5 km depth and a magnitude 4.1 at 1.9 km depth on 2026-02-12 — both very shallow — indicating elevated near-surface strain release in a populated region.
- A late-window uptick in daily event counts — 9 events on 2026-02-14 and 6 on 2026-02-15 — follows a mid-period lull of only 2 events each on 2026-02-06 and 2026-02-11, suggesting a non-uniform clustering pattern across the 14-day window.

## Risk Watch (Next 7 Days)
- **Tobelo, Indonesia** warrants priority monitoring given the combination of 8 total events, a magnitude 5.7 occurrence, and multiple shallow events at depths as low as 3.3 km; shallow events in this tectonically active zone indicate elevated potential for local ground damage if magnitudes increase.
- **Mohr, Iran** should remain under close watch: 3 of its 6 recorded events occurred at depths under 10 km, and crustal seismicity at these depths in a populated inland region suggests infrastructure stress that emergency coordinators should track against any escalation in magnitude band.
- **Nikolski, Alaska and the broader Aleutian arc** merit continued surveillance given 8 events at Nikolski plus additional events logged near the Aleutian Islands; while no tsunami flags were triggered in the current dataset, the arc's known tsunamigenic character makes any magnitude escalation above the current 5.2 peak a monitoring threshold of concern.
