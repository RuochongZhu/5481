# Earthquake Situation Report

## Executive Summary
106 seismic events were recorded globally between 2026-01-26 and 2026-02-16, with magnitudes ranging from 4.0 to 6.1 and an average of 4.64. Tobelo, Indonesia (12 events) and Nikolski, Alaska (11 events) led all regions by event frequency. A late-period uptick — 9 events on both 2026-02-03 and 2026-02-14 — suggests episodic clustering that warrants continued attention in the highest-frequency zones.

- **Tobelo, Indonesia** recorded 12 events over the 21-day window, the highest frequency of any single region; sampled events include a magnitude 5.7 at 152.9 km depth on 2026-02-14, indicating persistent seismic activity across a range of focal depths.
- **Nikolski, Alaska** produced 11 events, with sampled depths spanning from 11.4 km to 146.2 km, suggesting activity across multiple crustal and subcrustal levels; a magnitude 5.1 event at 14.2 km depth on 2026-02-14 indicates shallow rupture potential in this zone.
- **Mohr, Iran** registered 10 events, including a magnitude 4.9 at only 8.5 km depth on 2026-02-12 and a separate event at 1.9 km depth — both very shallow focal depths that elevate local ground-shaking concern relative to deeper events of equivalent magnitude.
- The period's maximum magnitude of **6.1** was recorded in the dataset, and daily counts peaked at **9 events on 2026-02-03 and again on 2026-02-14**, indicating two distinct episodes of elevated global seismic activity within the reporting window.

## Data Caveats

- The dataset is sourced from a **USGS synthetic provider** (variant\_id: variant\_02\_last\_21d) and applies a minimum magnitude filter of 4.0; sub-threshold events, which can be significant indicators of swarm behavior, are excluded, and the synthetic provenance means independent verification against authoritative USGS or regional network catalogs is advised before operational decisions are finalized.
