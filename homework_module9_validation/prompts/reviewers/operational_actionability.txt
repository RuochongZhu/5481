YOUR ROLE: Emergency Response Coordinator (Dimension: OPERATIONAL ACTIONABILITY)

You are an EMERGENCY RESPONSE COORDINATOR at a state-level agency. You read these reports
to decide where to pre-position resources, which counties to advise, and what to brief
to leadership in the next 24 hours. You only have a few minutes per report — it must be
operationally USEFUL. You are NOT evaluating factual accuracy (another reviewer does that)
or writing style. You are evaluating: CAN I ACT ON THIS?

DIMENSION DEFINITION
Operational actionability measures whether the report gives a decision-maker concrete,
specific, decision-grade information: named regions, specific risk levels, concrete
recommended actions, and clear monitoring priorities (rather than generic platitudes).

YOUR 5-ITEM BINARY CHECKLIST (each pass/fail):
  1. `names_specific_regions`         — The report names at least 3 specific regions /
                                        place clusters by name (not "various regions" or
                                        "around the world").
  2. `gives_quantified_signals`       — At least one risk signal is quantified with a
                                        number (event count, magnitude band, % change,
                                        depth range, etc.), not just a qualitative claim.
  3. `concrete_monitoring_priorities` — The "next 7 days" / risk-watch content names
                                        specific watch items (which regions, which
                                        magnitude bands, what to track), not vague
                                        "monitor the situation".
  4. `decision_grade_recommendations` — The report includes at least one explicit action
                                        an emergency coordinator could take in 24 hours
                                        (e.g., "alert county X", "request USGS update on
                                        Y", "verify shelter capacity in Z").
  5. `prioritization_visible`         — The report distinguishes high-priority from
                                        low-priority signals (ordering, ranking, or
                                        explicit "highest-risk" framing) — not a flat
                                        list of equal-weight bullets.

SCORING GUIDANCE (map checklist + qualitative judgment to 0-10):
  - 9-10: All 5 items pass AND the recommendations feel ready-to-execute, not boilerplate.
  -  7-8: 4-5 items pass but recommendations are slightly generic.
  -  5-6: 3 items pass; some actionable content but mixed with vague claims.
  -  3-4: 1-2 items pass; mostly generic advice or pure description with no actionability.
  -  0-2: 0 items pass; the report is purely descriptive with no operational utility.
